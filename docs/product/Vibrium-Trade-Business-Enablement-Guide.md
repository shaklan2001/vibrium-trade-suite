## 1. One-minute pitch

**Vibrium Trade** helps a **sourcing company** run the full journey of a product deal — from the buyer’s first enquiry to goods arriving and payment finishing — with **AI agents that do the repetitive work** and an **intelligence dashboard** that shows risks, delays, and what to do next.

A typical deal takes **4–6 months**. One missed follow-up, late sample, document error, or port delay can cost weeks and a lot of money. The platform exists so the client’s team spends less time chasing and more time deciding.

## 2. Who are the three parties?

Think of every deal as involving three roles:

| Role | Also called | What they want |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Importer** | Buyer, brand, retailer | Good product, right price, on time, safe quality |
| **Sourcing company** | Our client, trading house, buying agent | Match factories, manage process, earn fee/margin, keep both sides happy |
| **Manufacturer** | Exporter, factory, supplier | Orders, clear specs, payment, predictable production |

**Two ways deals run:**

1. **Managed** — Importer talks mainly to the sourcing company; sourcing company manages the factory end-to-end.  
2. **Referee** — Importer deals more directly with the factory; sourcing company is QC / escrow / “honest broker.”

Vibrium Trade supports both. In demos we usually show **Managed**.

## 3. The story of one deal (plain English)

Use this story when presenting. It matches the demo’s “golden path” tour.

### Chapter A — “I need this product”

A German home brand needs **15,000 premium cotton bedding sets** for Hamburg.  
They email the sourcing company (or raise a request in the portal).

This first formal enquiry is an **RFQ** (Request for Quotation).

**What Vibrium does:** Reads the email, extracts product / quantity / destination / certifications, and starts matching factories.

---

### Chapter B — “Who can make this?”

The sourcing company already knows hundreds of factories (China, Vietnam, India, etc.).  
They shortlist factories that can make bedding, have the right certificates (e.g. OEKO-TEX), and historically deliver on time.

**What Vibrium does:** **Sourcing Scout** (AI agent) searches the portfolio and ranks factories — not only on price, but on reliability.

---

### Chapter C — “Ask them for a price”

RFQs go out to shortlisted factories by **email and WhatsApp**, often in Chinese.

Some factories reply. Some go quiet.

**What Vibrium does:** **Supplier Chaser** follows up automatically (WhatsApp → voice if needed), in the supplier’s language, with human approval when needed.

---

### Chapter D — “Which quote is actually best?”

Quotes arrive with different currencies, shipping terms, minimum order quantities, and lead times.  
The cheapest unit price is **not** always the best choice — a “cheap” factory that is late can destroy the whole season.

**What Vibrium does:** **Quote Analyst** converts everything into a comparable **landed cost** and recommends with a clear reason (e.g. slightly higher price, but 96% on-time history).

---

### Chapter E — “Send samples, then order”

Buyer likes a factory → **samples** are sent and tested.  
If OK → factory issues a **PI** (Proforma Invoice).  
Buyer issues a **PO** (Purchase Order) and pays an **advance** (often ~30%).

**What Vibrium does:** Tracks samples, PI/PO, and payment milestones; reminds people before money is late.

---

### Chapter F — “Make it — but don’t go blind for 2 months”

Production starts. This phase is long and risky (festivals, capacity issues, quality problems).

Mid-way they may do **DUPRO** (during-production inspection).  
Near the end they do **PSI** (pre-shipment inspection) with an agency (SGS, Bureau Veritas, etc.).

**What Vibrium does:** **Production Sentinel** predicts delays early. **Inspection Planner** helps schedule QC. Humans approve expensive mitigations (e.g. overtime).

---

### Chapter G — “Ship it”

Cargo is booked on a vessel. Documents are prepared.  
When cargo is on the ship, a **BL** (Bill of Lading) is issued — this paper (or electronic equivalent) is the **key to the cargo**.

Ship sails Asia → Europe. Ports get congested. Containers have limited **free days** before **demurrage** (expensive daily charges).

**What Vibrium does:** **Logistics Sentinel** watches ETAs and port congestion. **Doc Checker** catches document mistakes before the bank/customs reject them.

---

### Chapter H — “Arrive, pay balance, release goods”

Ship arrives. Buyer pays the **balance**.  
Only then is the BL typically **released**, so the buyer can clear customs and take goods to their warehouse.

After sailing, a lot of import customs work is on the **buyer’s side** — but the sourcing company still cares about payment, docs, and reputation.

**What Vibrium does:** Tracks payment, BL release status, risks, and shows leadership the whole picture on one dashboard.

## 4. Acronym cheat sheet (memorize these first)

| Acronym | Full form | Say it like this in a meeting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **RFQ** | Request for Quotation | “The buyer’s enquiry — what they want and roughly at what price.” |
| **PI** | Proforma Invoice | “The factory’s formal offer. Advance payment is usually against this.” |
| **PO** | Purchase Order | “The buyer’s official order confirming they accept the deal.” |
| **BL / B/L** | Bill of Lading | “The shipping document that also acts as title to the goods. No BL release, no cargo.” |
| **CI** | Commercial Invoice | “The final commercial invoice for customs and payment.” |
| **PL** | Packing List | “What is packed in which cartons/containers.” |
| **COO** | Certificate of Origin | “Proof of which country the goods were made in.” |
| **LC** | Letter of Credit | “Bank-backed payment method with strict document rules.” |
| **TT** | Telegraphic Transfer | “Normal bank wire payment.” |
| **FOB / CIF / DDP…** | Incoterms | “Who pays for shipping, insurance, and where risk transfers.” |
| **PSI** | Pre-Shipment Inspection | “Final quality check before goods leave the factory/port.” |
| **DUPRO** | During Production Inspection | “Mid-production quality check.” |
| **AQL** | Acceptable Quality Limit | “How many defects are allowed in the sample check.” |
| **ETD / ETA** | Est. Departure / Arrival | “When the ship should leave / arrive.” |
| **OTD** | On-Time Delivery | “How often this supplier historically delivered on time.” |
| **HS Code** | Harmonized System code | “The customs classification number for the product.” |
| **MOQ** | Minimum Order Quantity | “Smallest quantity the factory will accept.” |
| **Demurrage** | — | “Penalty for leaving containers at port too long.” |

### Incoterms in one line

Buyers and factories argue less when Incoterms are clear:

- **EXW** — Buyer picks up at factory (buyer does almost everything).  
- **FOB** — Factory gets goods on ship at origin port (very common).  
- **CIF** — Factory covers cost, insurance, freight to destination port.  
- **DDP** — Delivered duty paid to buyer’s door (seller does almost everything).

You do **not** need to memorize all Incoterms for a demo — know that Vibrium **normalizes quotes across different Incoterms** so comparison is fair.

## 5. Why clients feel pain (use these talking points)

| Pain they feel today | What goes wrong | How to phrase it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chasing factories | WhatsApp/email ignored for days | “Your cycle dies in silence — not in negotiation.” |
| Wrong ‘cheap’ supplier | Late shipment, air freight, missed season | “Unit price is not landed risk.” |
| Long black box production | Surprise delay at day 90 | “You need a warning at day 40, not a crisis at day 120.” |
| Document errors | Bank rejects LC documents | “A typo on a consignee name can freeze a container.” |
| Port congestion / demurrage | Unexpected port charges | “Free days are a ticking clock.” |
| No single dashboard | Excel + calls + memory | “Leadership can’t see risk across 100 open deals.” |
| Buyer keeps asking ‘status?’ | Trust erodes | “Transparency is part of the product.” |

**Money framing:** On a multi-month deal worth hundreds of thousands of dollars, a **1–2 week delay** or a **demurrage incident** often costs more than the software.

## 8. Objection handling (business team FAQ)

**“Is this just ChatGPT on a dashboard?”**  
No. Numbers are calculated by rules/data first. AI explains and recommends using those facts. Risky messages need human approval.

**“Will it replace my sourcing managers?”**  
No. It removes chasing, formatting, status updates, and missed follow-ups — so managers negotiate and decide.

**“Our factories only use WeChat/WhatsApp.”**  
That’s exactly why multi-channel chase and bilingual templates matter. The manufacturer doesn’t need to log into our system on day one.

**“We already have ERP / Excel.”**  
ERP stores records. Excel stores hope. Vibrium Trade **orchestrates the work and warns about risk** across the timeline ERP doesn’t manage well.

**“What if AI sends something wrong to a factory?”**  
Default posture is **human-in-the-loop** for outreach and commercial actions. Approvals queue is a feature, not a footnote.

**“Can you white-label this as our product?”**  
Yes as a delivery option — confirm branding in the workshop. Demo today is Vibrium-branded.

**“Is the demo real data / real backend?”**  
Be honest: the public demo is a **high-fidelity product simulation** for visualization. Production connects to real channels, workflows, and Vibrium agent infrastructure. Don’t oversell the demo as live ERP.

## 9. Words to use / words to avoid

### Prefer
- Cycle time, landed cost, on-time delivery, free days, demurrage risk  
- Shortlist, chase, HITL / approval, grounded insights  
- Managed vs referee model  
- “Days of delay prevented”

### Avoid (until you know the room)
- Overly technical model names  
- “Fully autonomous AI runs your business”  
- Claiming customs brokerage replacement  
- Saying BL when you mean commercial invoice (different documents!)

## 10. Mini glossary of “sound smart” process phrases

| Phrase | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| “Advance against PI” | Buyer pays part payment based on proforma before production |
| “Balance against BL copy / LC” | Rest of money tied to shipping documents or bank LC terms |
| “Telex release” | BL surrendered electronically so cargo can be released without original paper BL |
| “Cut-off” | Last time to submit docs / containers for a sailing |
| “Transhipment” | Container changes mother vessel at a hub port |
| “Single-source risk” | Too much volume with one factory |
| “Landed cost” | True cost after freight, duty, etc. |
